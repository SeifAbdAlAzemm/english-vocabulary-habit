# English Vocabulary Habit — Mature Routine (V1.1)

> **Status:** Updated draft for review — learning schedule, research-based review protocol, and Minimum Viable Day clarified  
> **Learning source:** [IELTS Tutors — Academic Word List (AWL)](https://ieltstutors.org/academic-word-list/)  
> **Pilot duration:** 14 days  
> **Core principle:** **Learn → Produce → Get Feedback → Retrieve → Measure**

## 1. Goal

Build a sustainable daily habit to improve my English vocabulary, rather than merely increasing the number of words memorized. Successful learning means that I can **understand a word, remember it after some time, and use it independently in a new context**.

We distinguish between two goals:

- **Habit Goal:** Engage in a genuine vocabulary-related activity every day, even if it is only a short review.
- **Learning Goal:** Learn new words, retain them, and use them correctly and naturally.

We also distinguish between **Coverage** (the number of words I have studied) and **Mastery** (the number of words I can retrieve and use later).

## 2. Rules for the 14-Day Pilot

| Item | Decision |
|---|---|
| Vocabulary source | AWL on IELTS Tutors |
| Word order | In sequence within Sublist 1, then the remaining sublists; no random selection |
| Learning days | Sunday–Thursday |
| Learning rate | **Two new words per learning day** = 10 per week |
| Review days | Friday and Saturday are review-only days, with due reviews on the other days as well |
| Pilot target | **20 new words** in two weeks; also record the actual number learned |
| Daily consistency | Actual learning or review activity; a 5-minute review is acceptable on a busy day |
| Session duration | **Flexible**; no mandatory time limit during the pilot, and actual time is recorded |

**Fresh start:** Start from the beginning of AWL Sublist 1, from scratch. The first two words are `analysis` and `approach`. **Do not import the five words from the previous experiment, use them as a baseline, or count any previous reviews or achievements.** The 20 words count only if they are learned in new pilot sessions, even if I have encountered some of them before.

## 3. Daily Learning Session

### Step 1 — Review Due Words

1. Review words whose scheduled review dates have arrived **before** opening the answers or starting new words.
2. Look at the word alone and try to recall its primary meaning or explain it in your own words, without assistance.
3. Record the result of the **first attempt**: `Remembered` or `Forgot`.
4. After recording the result, reveal the meaning and example for feedback. Forgetting a word is normal, and it is useful to record it.

> The Day +7 review also includes a **new sentence**, as explained in the measurement section.

### Step 2 — Understand Each New Word

Repeat the following for each of the two words:

1. **Meaning & Part of Speech:** Understand the primary meaning and the word's grammatical category in the selected context.
2. **Pronunciation:** Listen to the pronunciation, say the word aloud (for example, three times), and then try to pronounce it without the audio.
3. **One Source Example:** Read one AWL example and understand how the word is used in it; notice a useful *collocation* if there is one.
4. **Useful Word Family (optional):** Record one useful related form when needed, not every derivative.
5. **One Synonym (optional):** Choose one synonym if it adds to your understanding, with a brief note on how its usage differs. Memorizing several synonyms is not required.

**Rule for unfamiliar words in examples:** Ignore unnecessary details and look up a word only if it prevents you from understanding the example. If a secondary word seems important, add it to the **Parking Lot** for later, rather than to today's target.

### Step 3 — Independent Personal Production

- Hide the definition and example, then write **one original sentence from your own life** for each word (work, learning, everyday situations, etc.).
- You may look at the target word only; do not copy the website's sentence or ask AI to write a ready-made sentence for you.
- Preserve the **First Attempt** as written, even if it contains mistakes. It is evidence of your actual level.
- Linking the two words in one context is **optional**; two natural sentences matter more than forcing the words into an artificial story.

### Step 4 — Feedback & Self-Correction

For each sentence:

1. Share **the word + your first sentence** with AI.
2. Ask it to evaluate your use of the word, then give **at most two hints** about the most important Grammar, Collocation, or Naturalness issues, **without showing the correction immediately**.
3. Try to correct the sentence yourself (**Second Attempt**).
4. After your attempt, request a corrected, natural version with a brief explanation of the changes. A more advanced version is optional **only if appropriate**; it is not a goal in itself.
5. Keep the First Attempt, Self-Correction, and Corrected Version separate. **Do not change the score of the first attempt** after correction.

**Quality rule:** **Correct → Natural → More sophisticated (only when useful)**. More difficult vocabulary is not necessarily better vocabulary.

### Step 5 — Quick Retrieval & Log

- Hide the information and try to remember the meanings of the two words once more at the end of the session.
- Record actual study time, new words, attempts and sentences, and important corrections.
- The end-of-session test is immediate practice; **do not confuse it with the Retention measure after one day or one week**.

## 4. Review Mechanism — Spaced Retrieval Protocol

### 4.1 Why this method? How strong is the evidence?

We rely on **two well-supported principles** rather than simply rereading examples:

1. **Spaced Practice:** Reviewing vocabulary across separate sessions is better for long-term retention than cramming reviews into one session. A comprehensive analysis of **317 experiments** found that the optimal spacing depends on the intended retention interval [Cepeda et al., 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16719566/).
2. **Retrieval Practice + Feedback:** Try to retrieve the answer from memory before revealing it, then correct mistakes. A foreign-language vocabulary experiment found a substantial effect of repeated retrieval on delayed recall [Karpicke & Roediger, 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18276894/). A study of a vocabulary-learning app (**79 participants**) found a **29-percentage-point difference** in favor of an appropriate combination of spacing, testing, and feedback compared with weaker learning conditions; **this is a finding from the study, not a guaranteed prediction of my results** [2021 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8638698/).

> **Important scientific limitation:** The `+1 / +3 / +7 / +14 / +30` intervals are **a practical experimental schedule, not a magic schedule proven optimal for everyone**. A study focused on second-language vocabulary found a limited advantage for expanding over equal spacing under certain conditions, while findings in the literature are mixed [Nakata, 2015](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/studies-in-second-language-acquisition/article/abs/effects-of-expanding-and-equal-spacing-on-second-language-vocabulary-learning/D1D796306985C52F9BE7A1200AC50DB9). We do not need a complex algorithm before collecting real data.

### 4.2 Fixed Review Schedule — Milestone Reviews

For each word, use the date it was first learned during the pilot as `Day 0`, and calculate the following **from that word's learning date**, regardless of the day of the week:

| Review date | Test type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Day 0 | Learn the word + first sentence + Quick Recall | Immediate practice; excluded from delayed-retention measurement |
| Day +1 | Meaning Recall | First attempt without assistance |
| Day +3 | Meaning Recall | First attempt without assistance |
| Day +7 | Meaning Recall + **a new, independent sentence** | One-week Retention and Production test |
| Day +14 | Meaning Recall | Continued review after the pilot |
| Day +30 | Meaning Recall | Longer-term measurement |

*Example:* If I learn `analysis` on Sunday, November 15, 2026, its scheduled tests will be Monday, November 16; Wednesday, November 18; Sunday, November 22; Sunday, November 29; and Tuesday, December 15. These are **illustrative dates**, not mandatory pilot start dates.

### 4.3 How does the system determine which words are due today?

A clear, programmable rule:

**Due Words = all words with an incomplete scheduled review whose due date ≤ today's date, including remedial reviews.**

- **Scheduled Review:** The main review date (+1/+3/+7/+14/+30) is calculated from the `Learn Date`.
- **Overdue:** The date has passed and the review has not been completed; it remains due until completed. Record both `Scheduled Date` and `Actual Date`.
- **Remedial Review:** If a word is forgotten during a test, add a remedial review **for the next day**. If it coincides with a scheduled milestone for that word, show one combined test card while preserving the type of each obligation in the log.
- **Priority:** Overdue reviews first, then those due today, then extra tests. Do not postpone words just because it is a new-word learning day.
- **No silent rescheduling:** A `Forgot` result does not delete or move the main reviews without a record. After the pilot, we may try a personalized algorithm based on results, but we will not change the scheduling system during the experiment.

### 4.4 The best way to conduct each review

1. **Hide:** Show **only the Target Word** (and, if genuinely needed to resolve ambiguity, its Part of Speech); do not show the meaning, example, or synonyms.
2. **Retrieve:** Explain the primary meaning in words that work for you (Arabic or English) **before** revealing the answer. On Day +7, also write **a new sentence** before seeing any hint or correction.
3. **Record First Attempt:** Mark `Remembered` if you can recall the intended meaning accurately enough without help; otherwise mark `Forgot`. Score the Day +7 sentence as 0/1/2 according to the rubric.
4. **Reveal & Feedback:** Reveal the definition, and revisit the example or usage pattern if needed. If you were wrong, look at the correct answer; **do not simply repeat the mistake**.
5. **Repair:** For a forgotten word, try to retrieve it again after going through other words or at the end of the session. Log this as **extra practice**, which does not change the first test result. Add a review for the following day.

**The purpose of reviewing is retrieval, not counting how many times you read a word.** Do not factor more sophisticated wording or vocabulary into the Recall score if the primary meaning is correct.

### 4.5 Measurement integrity rules

- `Forgot` = I tried to recall the word and failed; `Missed` = I did not take the test at all; `Late` = I took it after its scheduled date. Do not confuse the three.
- Only the first attempt counts; repeating the same card after feedback is practice, not a retroactive correct answer.
- Preserve original scheduled test dates and the difference between those dates and actual completion dates.
- **Day +1** and **Day +7** reports are based on Milestone tests. Separate late completions from on-time completions, and display the sample size and incomplete tests. Remedial reviews are not included in the denominator for these indicators.
- A review on Friday or Saturday is no different scientifically from a review on any other day; the calendar determines **new-word learning days**, but **each word's due date determines its reviews**.

## 5. Measurement Contract — What Do We Measure, and How?

### A. Retention

Can I remember a word's meaning without assistance **after one day and after one week**?

`Recall Rate = Correct First Recalls / Completed Scheduled Recall Tests × 100`

- Display **Day +1** and **Day +7** separately.
- Alongside the rate, display **the number of completed tests out of those due**, so missing reviews do not hide behind a high percentage.
- Late reviews remain visible with their dates and are not automatically treated as tests completed on time.

### B. Production

Can I write **a new sentence** using the word, without relying on an example or AI? Score the first attempt as follows:

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **0 — Incorrect** | The word is missing, or its meaning/usage is inappropriate |
| **1 — Needs Improvement** | The meaning is understandable, but the word's usage or collocation needs a clear correction |
| **2 — Correct & Natural** | The word is used with an appropriate meaning and natural structure; sophisticated language or perfection in every unrelated grammatical detail is not required |

`Production Accuracy = Day +7 tests scored 2 / Completed Day +7 production tests × 100`

- Record **Day 0 Production** as a separate initial assessment for later comparison.
- Use the same rubric throughout the experiment and retain AI's reason for each score; this is an educational indicator and may require human review.

### C. Time

`Average Active Session Time = Total Active Minutes / Completed Sessions`

- Record actual minutes of active practice, **excluding interruptions**.
- Show the average for **Learning Sessions** separately from **Review-only Sessions**.
- During the experiment, there is no strict time target; the priority is whether the duration is realistic and repeatable.

### D. Consistency

`Consistency = Active Days / 14 × 100`

- An active day = **recorded** genuine learning or retrieval/review activity (including a completed MVD); merely opening the app does not count.
- On a busy day, completing the **Minimum Viable Day (MVD)** as specified in Section 6 is enough to count the day as active, even if two new words were not completed.

### E. Review Completion (Supporting Metric)

`Review Completion = Completed Scheduled Reviews / Reviews Due by Report Date × 100`

Also track **late reviews** separately from those completed on time, and exclude `Extra Practice` and `Remedial` tests from the official Milestone denominator. Count each scheduled review's completion only once.

## 6. Flexibility and Minimum Viable Day (MVD)

**MVD = the smallest genuine practice activity that preserves the habit on a busy day, without pretending that a full session was completed.** The approximate target is **5 minutes**, not a mandatory timer.

### MVD — Definition of Done

Follow **the path that matches your situation** and log the result:

| Situation | What must actually be completed |
|---|---|
| There are **Due / Overdue** words | Test recall of **two different words** without assistance (or all due words if fewer than two), record `Remembered/Forgot`, then reveal the answers and correct mistakes. |
| No words are due, but some words have been learned before | Test the meanings of **two previously learned words** from memory, or one word plus a new short sentence. Log as **Extra Practice**, not a scheduled review. |
| It is genuinely the first day, with no words learned yet | Learn **one word**: primary meaning + one example + an attempt at a personal sentence. Record it as one new word, and count its learning date as Day 0. |

Then record **the date, session type (`MVD`), words/tests completed, actual minutes, and attempt results**. Completing these requirements = an active day for Consistency; it does not mean the two-word target or every review due that day was completed.

**Not required for an MVD:** Learning two new words, Synonyms, Word Families, upgrading a sentence to an advanced version, or completing all Due Words if time does not allow. Anything unfinished remains due.

### Other Flexibility Rules

- **Missed Learning Day:** Do not double the number of words the next day to catch up; continue from the last word, and record actual progress against the target.
- **Difficult Word:** Review it more often while keeping the first-attempt result unchanged; do not mix Extra Practice with official metrics.
- **Optional Weekly Challenge:** Write a short paragraph using previously learned words (3–5 words if possible). Linking two words every day is not mandatory.
- **Parking Lot:** Note secondary words or questions to revisit without turning them into extra homework.
- **Weekend Rule:** Friday and Saturday are for review, with no new words planned, unless an exceptional day calls for it; record what was actually achieved without falsifying the schedule.

## 7. Minimum Logging Requirements (Excel Now, App Later)

| Record | Required Fields |
|---|---|
| **Word** | Word, Learn Date, Meaning, Part of Speech, Useful Phrase/Context, First Attempt, Self-Correction, Corrected Version |
| **Review Attempt** | Word, Review Type (`Milestone` / `Remedial` / `Extra`), Checkpoint (+1/+3/+7/+14/+30 if applicable), Scheduled Date, Actual Date, First Recall Result, Production Score (if applicable), Was Late |
| **Daily Session** | Date, Type (`Learning` / `Review` / `MVD`), Active Minutes, New Words Count, Scheduled Reviews Completed |

Synonym, Word Family, and Parking Lot fields are **optional**. Success is not measured by how many fields are filled in.

## 8. End-of-Pilot Evaluation

| Metric | Initial Guideline |
|---|---:|
| Day +7 Recall Rate | **≥ 80%** |
| Day +7 Production Accuracy | **≥ 70%** |
| Review Completion | **≥ 80%** |
| Consistency | **≥ 12 / 14 days** |
| Time | Realistic and manageable for me; no predefined numeric target |

These are **experimental decision guidelines**, not scientifically established pass/fail thresholds. Review the results together with their context:

- If learning remains consistent, retrieval and usage are good, and the time commitment feels manageable: **try 15 words per week**.
- If the workload feels heavy or the results are weak: investigate the cause (time, examples, review, Production, etc.) and adjust **based on data**, not impressions alone.
- **Important:** Some words learned in the second week will not have reached Day +7 by the end of Day 14; report only tests that are eligible by then and complete the remaining tests on their due dates. Separate late tests from on-time tests. Reviews at +14 and +30 continue afterward.

## 9. What Is Not Required in V1.1

- Memorizing every Synonym or Word Family for each word.
- Understanding and memorizing every secondary word in the website's examples.
- Forcing sophisticated vocabulary or a single paragraph containing all the day's words.
- Achieving a word-count target at the expense of understanding or retrieval.
- Building lots of app features or an intelligent review algorithm before testing this workflow in practice.
